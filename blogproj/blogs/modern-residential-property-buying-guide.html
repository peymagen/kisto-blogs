Modern Residential Property Buying Guide

Looking for a modern flat can be exciting, but the best shortlist comes from looking beyond photographs. This guide walks you through layout, location, total cost, site visits and documents so you can compare a property with greater clarity.

KISTO SONS

Start with the way you want to live. Think about bedrooms, usable space, preferred floor, parking, location and the people who will use the home every day.

Create a practical requirement list before you start visiting properties. It helps you separate genuine requirements from features that simply look attractive in a brochure.

Check the Layout and Usable Space

During a site visit, look closely at room sizes, kitchen placement, bathrooms, balconies, storage and movement between spaces. A thoughtful layout can make everyday living more comfortable.

Imagine your furniture and routine inside the flat. If you are comparing two properties, use the same checklist for both instead of judging one by photographs and the other by a visit.

Location Should Work for Everyday Life

Consider the routes you will actually use for work, school, healthcare, shopping and public transport. A convenient daily journey can matter long after the excitement of buying has passed.

Visit the surrounding area instead of relying only on an online listing. Check the approach road, parking, traffic and nearby facilities at a practical time of day.

Understand Price and Total Purchase Cost

Compare the quoted flat price with the complete purchase cost. Depending on the property, this can include applicable registration or transaction costs, maintenance, parking, furnishing and other charges.

If you plan to use finance, understand the expected monthly repayment and keep enough room in your household budget before finalising the property.

Inspect the Flat Before You Decide

Look at natural light, ventilation, flooring, doors, windows, plumbing, electrical points and other visible details during the visit. Small observations can help you compare properties more realistically.

Take notes or photographs with the seller's or developer's permission. This gives you something practical to refer back to when comparing several shortlisted flats.

Verify Documents and Transaction Terms

Before paying a major amount or signing, review the ownership, approvals, agreement and other property-specific records that apply to the transaction.

If an important point is unclear, ask for clarification and consider independent professional advice where appropriate.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I compare flats only by price?

Price matters, but compare it with usable area, location, parking, layout, condition and the complete cost rather than looking at the headline number alone.

Should I visit a flat before booking?

A site visit is valuable because photographs cannot show every practical detail. Check the space, surroundings, access and common areas for yourself.

What information should I ask the property consultant for?

Ask for the property details, current price breakup, available configuration and applicable terms for the specific flat before moving forward.
