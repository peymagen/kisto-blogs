Things to Check Before Buying a New Flat

Buying a new flat is a big decision, but the process becomes easier when you know exactly what to check. Use this practical checklist before you book so you can compare properties with confidence.

KISTO SONS

Start with the exact configuration: bedrooms, usable area, floor, balcony or utility space and parking, if applicable. Make sure you understand what is included in the quoted property details.

Ask for the relevant floor plan and specification so you can compare the flat with other options on the same basis.

Understand the Price Breakup

Ask for a clear explanation of the quoted price and applicable charges. Depending on the property, these can include registration-related costs, maintenance, parking, utilities or other project-specific expenses.

Keep the complete cost in writing. A clear price breakup makes it much easier to compare two flats without being distracted by different ways of presenting the price.

Visit the Actual Property or Project

Photos and sample units can help, but a site visit gives you practical information about the approach road, surroundings, common areas and the actual feel of the space.

Check light, ventilation, room proportions and access to lifts, stairs and parking where applicable. Look at the things you will experience every day.

Ask About Handover and Possession

Understand the expected handover or possession terms stated for the property and the conditions attached to them.

Read the relevant agreement rather than relying only on verbal timelines. Keep a copy of the documents you sign.

Check Project and Property Documents

Ask for the documents and approvals relevant to the property and project. Requirements can vary, so confirm what applies to the specific flat.

For legal questions, independent professional verification can be useful, especially when an important clause or document is unclear.

Compare Before You Book

Compare a few suitable options using the same points: total cost, configuration, usable space, location, documents and site-visit observations.

A good comparison helps you choose based on the complete property rather than on a single feature such as price or appearance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I book immediately after a site visit?

Take time to understand the price, documents, terms and total cost before making a booking decision.

What is more important: price or location?

Both price and configuration matter, but they should be compared with your daily requirements and overall budget.

What should I keep after booking?

Keep copies of booking documents, receipts, written communications and the final terms connected with the property.
